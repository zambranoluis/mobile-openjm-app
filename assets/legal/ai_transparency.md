# AI Transparency Notice

> Version 1.1 · Effective September 1st, 2026

Published by **Open Jamaica AI Limited** under **Article 50 of the EU AI Act**
(Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), whose transparency obligations apply from **2 August 2026**.

## 1. You are interacting with an AI system

Every response from OpenJM is generated by a large language model. No human writes them,
and no human reads your conversations — the only automated review is the content
screening described in Section 4.

**Model output can be wrong.** It can state false things confidently, invent citations,
misread a document you have uploaded, and give different answers to the same question.
Do not rely on it for medical, legal, financial or safety-critical decisions without a
qualified person checking the result.

## 2. Obligations if you build on our API

If you build a client on the OpenJM API and put it in front of people in the EU, the
Article 50 duty applies to you as well as to us. Your interface must make clear, in the
interface itself and at the point of first interaction, that the user is interacting with
an AI system.

## 3. Marking of AI-generated content

Article 50(2) requires output from a generative AI system to be marked in a
machine-readable way so that it can be detected as artificially generated. That
requirement applies from **2 August 2026**.

The Service generates **text and embeddings only**. It does not generate synthetic
images, audio or video, so no deepfake-disclosure duty under Article 50(4) is currently
in scope.

We will publish our marking method for generated text here, and will not ship any image,
audio or video generation feature before content provenance marking is in place for it.

## 4. Automated decisions

**Content screening.** Requests and generated responses are screened automatically
against our [Acceptable Use Policy](acceptable_use_policy.md) by a classifier. A block
affects the individual request. Any decision that restricts your **account** is taken by
a person — see Section 6.

**Abuse blocking.** A request to a trap URL that no legitimate client visits blocks the
originating IP address for 7 days. This is applied automatically. It is a network-level
block rather than a judgement about you as a person, and it is reversible — contact us
and we will review it.

Because addresses can be shared, a block may occasionally affect someone who did
nothing. That is why it expires and why it is reversible on request.

**Long-term memory involves profiling.** If you enable it, a model reads your
conversations and derives durable facts about you — preferences, working style,
recurring goals — and reuses them in later answers. It is off unless you turn it on, and
you can list, export and delete every note.

**Quotas and rate limits** are automated, and determine only how quickly requests are
served.

## 5. Training

**We do not train on your data.** Not on prompts, responses, uploaded files, or code you
run. Nothing you send is collected for model training, so there is nothing to opt out
of.

## 6. Human oversight

Decisions that restrict an account rather than an address — suspension, key revocation,
termination — require human review, and a permanent termination requires approval by two
people.

You can ask for a human to review any automated decision that affects you, and you can
appeal an enforcement decision within 14 days under Section 6 of the
[Acceptable Use Policy](acceptable_use_policy.md).
